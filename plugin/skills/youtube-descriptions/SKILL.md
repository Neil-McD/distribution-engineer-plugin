# Write a YouTube description

A description has one job for the business: turn a viewer who cares into a click on the owner's link. Its second job is
helping the right people find the video in search. You write the two parts that do the second job well. Code owns the rest.

## What you write, and what you don't

You write exactly two parts:
1. **The summary:** 2–3 sentences.
2. **The chapters:** only for videos longer than about 8 minutes.

You never write:
- **links or addresses.** The owner's tracked link goes on the first line, added by code. A link you write would credit
  nothing.
- **hashtags, tags, a socials block or "subscribe".**
- **credits or disclosures.** Code keeps the ones already in the description.

## The summary

What the viewer gets from this video, in the owner's voice.

- **The first sentence carries it.** Viewers see only the first lines before "Show more", and search shows the start. Say
  what the video gives, not that it exists. Not "In this video I talk about...", but what they'll understand or be able to do.
- **Use the title's one or two main words,** the way someone would search for them. Write for the viewer, never a list of
  keywords.
- **Only what the video says.** Every claim, number and name comes from the transcript. Never invent a result, a guest,
  a promise or a price. If the transcript is missing, say so instead of writing.
- **Sound like the owner:** their words and rhythm, from the transcript and their voice profile. Avoid:
  - "in today's fast-paced world", "dive in", "game-changer", "unlock";
  - questions for engagement;
  - em-dash chains;
  - emoji, unless the owner uses them.
- **At most 300 characters.**

## The chapters

Only when the video is longer than about 8 minutes and its transcript has timings. A shorter video doesn't need them, and
YouTube can generate chapters itself.

- **YouTube's rules:**
  - the first chapter is `00:00`;
  - at least 3 chapters, in increasing order;
  - each at least 10 seconds long.

  Break any of these and YouTube shows none.
- **One chapter per real change of topic,** usually every 2–6 minutes. Not one per paragraph.
- **The time is where the topic starts in the transcript,** to the second. Never estimate it.
- **Titles are plain words a viewer would scan for,** 2–6 words, in the owner's language. Nothing clever that hides the
  topic.
- **Format:** one chapter per line, `MM:SS Title`, or `H:MM:SS Title` past an hour.
- **Existing chapters:** if the description already has chapters that follow these rules and fit the video, keep them
  exactly (answer `keep`).
- **No chapters needed:** answer `none`.

## How to answer

When the task asks for these as fields, answer `summary` with the summary text and `chapters` with the chapter lines, or
`keep` or `none`. Give a reason for any video you couldn't write: no transcript, or a transcript that doesn't match the
title.

## Check before you answer

- Would a viewer who reads only the first sentence know what they get?
- Is every claim in the transcript?
- Do the chapters start at `00:00`, number at least 3, and each run at least 10 seconds?
- No links, hashtags or socials?
